# Business Systems Case Study

## From Pen & Paper to a Structured Business Operation

**Real-world business systems implementation | Welkom, Free State, South Africa**

> **Project Type:** Business Systems Analysis & Design
> **Business:** Lebo's Cleaning Services
> **Tools:** Microsoft Excel, Data Analysis, Financial Modelling
> **Focus Areas:** Operations, Client Management, Pricing, Payroll, Scheduling & Financial Analysis

---

## 📌 Project Overview

Lebo's Cleaning Services is a real, operating small service business in Welkom, Free State.

As the business grew beyond its initial client base, its administration remained largely dependent on **pen-and-paper records, manual calculations and informal processes**.

I worked directly with the business owner to analyse how the business operated and designed a structured management system to bring its **clients, revenue, pricing, payroll, scheduling and financial information** into one organised framework.

This project demonstrates the practical application of **business systems analysis, data management, financial modelling and process improvement** within a real small-business environment.

---

# The Challenge

The business had genuine momentum, with **15+ active clients and a working team of employees**, but its operational processes had not developed at the same pace.

Key challenges included:

* Client and payment information being recorded manually
* No centralised view of active clients and recurring revenue
* Pricing decisions being made without a structured cost model
* Payroll dependent on manually tracking employee attendance
* Limited visibility into actual profitability
* No structured method for analysing labour and operating costs
* Scheduling that did not systematically account for travel between clients
* Lack of professional, standardised invoicing and receipts

The fundamental problem was not a lack of business activity.

**The business was growing faster than its management system.**

---

# What I Built

I designed and implemented an integrated operational system covering several core areas of the business.

### 1. Client & Revenue Management

Created a centralised Excel-based system for managing:

* Recurring clients
* Once-off jobs
* Client information
* Lead sources
* Payment status
* Revenue tracking

This replaced scattered manual records with a structured source of operational information.

---

### 2. Data-Driven Pricing Model

Developed a structured pricing model based on **garden/service size tiers and local market conditions**.

The objective was to move pricing away from intuition and toward a repeatable framework that could account for:

* Service size
* Labour requirements
* Operating costs
* Market conditions
* Desired profitability

This provides a more consistent basis for future pricing decisions.

---

### 3. Payroll System

Built a payroll structure around the way the business actually compensates employees.

Employee attendance is categorised as:

* **Full Day**
* **Half Day**
* **Absent**

The system then uses attendance information to calculate the corresponding employee pay.

This creates a more consistent and auditable payroll process while reducing manual calculation.

---

### 4. Financial Breakeven & Profitability Model

Developed a financial model linking revenue and operating costs to provide visibility into the business's financial position.

The model allows the owner to evaluate:

* Revenue
* Labour costs
* Operating expenses
* Cash position
* Breakeven requirements
* Labour-cost scenarios
* Required revenue for greater financial self-sufficiency

Instead of simply asking:

> **"How much money came in?"**

the business can now begin answering:

> **"How much did we actually make, what are our costs, and what does the business need to generate to become more profitable?"**

---

### 5. Route-Optimised Scheduling

Designed a **two-week rotating schedule** that groups clients geographically.

The objective was to:

* Reduce unnecessary travel
* Minimise fuel consumption
* Reduce wasted employee time
* Increase the number of jobs that can be completed per day
* Create a more predictable operating schedule

This demonstrates how operational data can be used not only for reporting, but also for **process optimisation**.

---

### 6. Professional Invoicing & Receipts

Created structured, branded and numbered business documents for:

* Recurring invoices
* Once-off service receipts
* Client payment records

The system separates recurring billing from once-off transactions and provides a more professional experience for clients.

# Business Systems Analysis Approach

This project followed a practical business-systems approach:

### 1. Understand

Analyse how the business currently operates.

### 2. Identify

Identify information gaps, manual processes and operational inefficiencies.

### 3. Structure

Determine what information needs to be captured and how it should relate to other business activities.

### 4. Design

Design the workflows, calculations, schedules and management structures required.

### 5. Implement

Build the system and integrate the different operational components.

### 6. Analyse

Use the resulting data to provide financial and operational insights.

### 7. Improve

Identify opportunities for further automation, optimisation and scalability.

# Scaling the System

The underlying system can be extended as the business grows beyond a single location.

For example, if the business expands from **Welkom into Bloemfontein or another city**, the system would need additional functionality.

### Multi-Branch Tracking

Clients, employees, revenue and costs could be tagged by:

* Branch
* City
* Service area

This would allow management to view each location independently as well as the business as a whole.

### Corporate Client Management

Larger corporate accounts may introduce:

* Formal quotations
* Contract terms
* Net payment terms
* Longer payment cycles

The system could therefore incorporate more formal accounts-receivable processes.

### Accounts Receivable

An ageing structure could track outstanding invoices by:

* Current
* 30 days
* 60+ days overdue

This becomes increasingly important when payment is received weeks after the service has been delivered.

### Branch-Level Profitability

Each location could have its own profitability analysis to determine whether a particular branch is independently sustainable.

### Payroll & Compliance

As employee numbers increase, payroll administration becomes more complex and may require additional statutory and accounting processes. These requirements should be managed in conjunction with the business's accountant.

### Cross-City Operations

Expansion into another city would likely require a dedicated local team or operational lead rather than relying on one crew to travel between locations.
